Energy-space absorption: the mathematics

Three questions: the change of variables ε → E, the momentum transfer q and the dirty approximation, and the two gap values Δ and Δ′
Model and notation of HHM, arXiv:1801.08988 (Δ̄ks = Δ0 − Δsεks/4t, Eq. 37; Eks = √(ξ² + |Δks|²), Eq. 26); absorption formalism of arXiv:2607.02812. Equations are numbered (1), (2), …; “state a” = (k,s), “state b” = (k′,s′).
Answers in one line each. (1) The sum over states can be written over ε or over E: it is the same integral (two branches, Jacobian E/κℓ, limits Emin≤E≤Emax). What is not equivalent is imposing energy conservation on ε instead of E. (2) q enters only through the Kronecker delta δ𝐤,𝐤′+𝐪 of the perturbation; the dirty approximation replaces it by its average 1/Nk, so 𝐤 and 𝐤′ are summed independently and q disappears (it survives only in the validity condition qℓ≪1). (3) There is one gap function Δ¯(ε); Δ and Δ′ are its values at the band energies of the two states. In energy space each state has two branches, Δσ(E)=Δ*−σbℓ/κ, and ξξ′+ΔΔ′=Emin2+σσ′ℓℓ′ for η=−1.

0. Notation

Δ¯(ε)=Δ0−bε with b=Δs/4t (HHM Eq. 37), ξ=ε−μ, so that Δ¯=ΔF−bξ with ΔF=Δ0−bμ. Put κ=1+b2, Emin=|ΔF|/κ, ε*=μ+bΔF/κ2, Δ*=ΔF/κ2, ξ*=ε*−μ=bΔ*. N(ε)=Nk−1∑𝐤sδ(ε−ε𝐤s) is the normal density of states of both helicity bands (∫Ndε=2, zero for |ε|>W), so ∑𝐤sF(ε𝐤s)=Nk∫dεN(ε)F(ε). The quasiparticle energy depends on (𝐤,s) only through ε (HHM Eq. 26 with |Δ𝐤s|=|Δ¯𝐤s|):

E(ε)2=ξ2+Δ¯(ε)2=(1+b2)ξ2−2bΔFξ+ΔF2=Emin2+κ2(ε−ε*)2
(1)

1. Integral over ε or over E?

Step 1 — the map ε↦E is two-to-one.

By (1), E≥Emin with the minimum at ε*. For every E>Emin there are exactly two band energies, one on each side of ε*:

εσ(E)=ε*+σℓκ,ℓ≡E2−Emin2,σ=±1,|dEdε|εσ=κ2|ε−ε*|E=κℓE
(2)
Step 2 — insert 1=∫Emin∞dEδ(E−E(ε)).

With δ(E−E(ε))=∑σδ(ε−εσ(E))/|dE/dε|εσ:

∑𝐤sF(ε𝐤s)=Nk∫dεN(ε)F(ε)=Nk∫EminEmaxdE∑σ=±νσ(E)F(εσ(E)),νσ(E)=N(εσ(E))EκE2−Emin2
(3)

This is an identity (a change of variables), not an approximation. The factor E/κℓ is the Jacobian |dε/dE|; it diverges like (E−Emin)−1/2 at the lower limit, which is integrable (it is the BCS density-of-states peak) and absent in the ε-form, where dE=(κℓ/E)dε→0 cancels it. Limits: the lower limit Emin is the minimum of (1). The upper limit is ∞ only for an unbounded band; the lattice band has N(ε)=0 for |ε|>W, so νσ(E)=0 whenever |εσ(E)|>W (the band test), i.e. effectively E≤Emax=max{E(−W),E(W)} (Emin=0.578t, Emax=7.89t in the example of the code review).

Step 3 — the absorbed power.

The golden rule with a constant matrix element B (Sec. 2) gives, for quasiparticle scattering (a = upper state, Ea=Eb+ℏω) and for pair creation (Ea+Eb=ℏω, b the lower member, Eb<Ea),

Wsc=2πωB2∑a,bCab[f(Eb)−f(Ea)]δ(Ea−Eb−ℏω),Wpair=2πωB2∑a,bEb<EaCabp[1−f(Ea)−f(Eb)]δ(Ea+Eb−ℏω)
(4)

with f(E)=1/(eE/T+1) (E measured from the condensate: no μ). Applying (3) to both sums and using the delta function to remove Ea:

Wsc=2πωB2Nk2∫EminEmax−ℏωdE∑σσ′νσ′(E+ℏω)νσ(E)Cσ′σ(E+ℏω,E)[f(E)−f(E+ℏω)]
(5)
Wpair=2πωB2Nk2∫Eminℏω/2dE∑σσ′νσ(E)νσ′(ℏω−E)Cσσ′p(E,ℏω−E)[1−f(E)−f(ℏω−E)]
(6)

In (5) the energy conservation δ(Ea−Eb−ℏω) and the occupations act on the quasiparticle energies; the sums over σ,σ′ run over the admissible branches. The ε-form used in the code is (5)–(6) written with ∫dεbN(εb) instead of ∫dE∑σνσ(E): by (3) the two are equal. Numerically: (3) holds to 7×10−5 – 1×10−3 for three test functions, and the literal E-integrals (5)–(6) agree with the ε-code to 1×10−4 (scattering) and 5×10−4 (pair creation).

What is not equivalent. The formula of the old code is ∫dεN(ε)N(ε+ℏω)C~[fN(ε)−fN(ε+ℏω)]: the delta function is δ(εa−εb−ℏω) and the occupation is the normal-state fN. Since E(ε) in (1) is nonlinear and two-to-one, no change of variables turns δ(εa−εb−ℏω) into δ(Ea−Eb−ℏω): it is a different integral, not a different parametrisation of (5). Check: for b=0 and a flat N(ε)=N0 one has νσ(E)=N0E/ℓ and (5) gives the textbook Ws/Wn→2f(Δ) as ω→0 (verified numerically to 10−4).

2. Where is q? What is the dirty approximation?

Step 1 — where q comes from.

A sound wave of wavevector 𝐪 and frequency ω couples to the electrons through H′(t)=H^′e−iωt+h.c. with

H^′=∑𝐤𝐤′∑ss′B(𝐤s|𝐤′s′)a𝐤s†a𝐤′s′,B(𝐤s|𝐤′s′)=B𝐪⟨𝐤s|𝐤′s′⟩δ𝐤,𝐤′+𝐪
(7)

(⟨𝐤s|𝐤′s′⟩ is the helicity-spinor overlap, ω=vs|𝐪|.) The quasiparticle matrix element is Mab=B(a|b)[uaub−ηvavb], so |Mab|2=|B(a|b)|2Cab with Cab=12[1+ξξ′/EE′−ηΔ¯Δ¯′/EE′]. Clean limit (momentum conserved): the Kronecker delta removes the sum over 𝐤′, and (4) becomes

Wsc(𝐪)=2πω|B𝐪|2∑𝐤ss′|⟨𝐤s|𝐤−𝐪,s′⟩|2C(𝐤s;𝐤−𝐪,s′)[f(E𝐤−𝐪,s′)−f(E𝐤s)]δ(E𝐤s−E𝐤−𝐪,s′−ℏω)
(8)

Here q appears in three places: in the arguments 𝐤−𝐪 of ε,E,Δ¯,u,v (hence in C), in the helicity overlap, and in the energy condition; for small q, E𝐤s−E𝐤−𝐪,s≈ℏ𝐯g(𝐤)·𝐪, and with ω=vsq the resonance condition is 𝐯g·𝐪^=vs. This is a 𝐤-resolved calculation, not an energy-space one.

Step 2 — the dirty approximation.

Statement. The paper averages B over the angles of 𝐤,𝐤′ and the spin indices and treats it as a constant (following its reference to Tinkham, Ch. 3); it does not use the word “dirty”, which is my name for the physical situation in which such an average is justified. Heuristically: with elastic impurity scattering of mean free path ℓmfp the states are no longer plane waves, and for qℓmfp≪1 (and ℓmfp≪ξ0, the usual dirty limit) the phonon cannot select 𝐤′=𝐤−𝐪. Mathematically the approximation replaces the Kronecker delta in (7) by its average over 𝐪, Nk−1∑𝐪δ𝐤,𝐤′+𝐪=1/Nk, i.e. the matrix element becomes independent of 𝐤,𝐤′:

∑𝐤𝐤′|B𝐪|2δ𝐤,𝐤′+𝐪F(𝐤,𝐤′)⟶|B𝐪|2Nk∑𝐤𝐤′F(𝐤,𝐤′)=|B𝐪|2Nk∑𝐪′∑𝐤F(𝐤,𝐤−𝐪′)
(9)

The right-hand side is the clean result (8) summed over all momentum transfers 𝐪′ with equal weight. In this model F depends on (𝐤,s) and (𝐤′,s′) only through ε,ε′ (the coherence factor C and the occupations) and through the helicity overlap. The overlap averages to 12 over each constant-energy contour, because 𝐠^−𝐤=−𝐠^𝐤 while ε𝐤s is even in 𝐤:

⟨12(1+ss′𝐠^𝐤·𝐠^𝐤′)⟩ε,ε′=12(1+ss′⟨𝐠^𝐤⟩ε·⟨𝐠^𝐤′⟩ε′)=12
(10)

Therefore the sums factorise into energy integrals, with B2=|B𝐪|2/2Nk:

W=2πωB2Nk2∫dεdε′N(ε)N(ε′)C(ε,ε′)[f(E′)−f(E)]δ(E−E′−ℏω)
(11)

which is (5) after Step 2 of Sec. 1, and which has the same B2 in WN, so B2 cancels in W/WN. Where is q now? In no formula: it has been averaged over. The physical value q=ω/vs appears only in the validity condition qℓmfp≪1; a number such as qa≈43 for ℏω=0.7t is a statement about that condition (and about the lattice zone), not an input. In the all-pairs sum the absorbed weight is spread over all momentum transfers: 10 %, 50 % and 90 % of it lies at |q|a<0.67, 1.85, 2.91 (zone corner 4.44).

What this is, and is not. (9) is an assumption of the paper's formalism; I did not derive its range of validity for the Rashba model. If you want a fixed 𝐪 (clean limit, qℓmfp≫1) you need (8), which is 𝐤-resolved. One supporting fact: the pairing kernel of HHM is Vss′∝U−2(Δt/t)(ε𝐤s+ε𝐤′s′) (Eq. 20 with 8Δts𝐤+4(Δt/t)VSOsR𝐤=−2(Δt/t)ε𝐤s), so all inputs depend on a state only through its band energy: energy is the natural label once momentum is not conserved.

3. Two gap values, Δ and Δ′

There is one gap function, Δ¯(ε)=ΔF−bξ (HHM Eq. 37). In the coherence factor C=12[1+ξξ′/EE′−ηΔΔ′/EE′] the unprimed quantities belong to state a and the primed ones to state b: Δ=Δ¯(εa), Δ′=Δ¯(εb) — the same function at two arguments. (If you mean the complex Δ𝐤s=se−iθ𝐤Δ¯𝐤s: |Δ𝐤s|=|Δ¯𝐤s|, and the phase cancels against the time-reversal phase of the helicity vertex, see the helicity note.)

Step 1 — in energy space each state has two branches.

Evaluate ξ=ε−μ and Δ¯=ΔF−bξ at the roots (2): with ξσ=ξ*+σℓ/κ and ξ*=bΔ*,

ξσ(E)=ξ*+σℓκ,Δσ(E)=Δ¯(εσ(E))=ΔF−bξσ=Δ*−σbℓκ,ξσ2+Δσ2=Emin2+ℓ2=E2
(12)

(The cross term 2σ(ℓ/κ)(ξ*−bΔ*) in ξσ2+Δσ2 vanishes, and ξ*2+Δ*2=κ2Δ*2=Emin2.) For energies Ea=Eb+ℏω there are four combinations (σa,σb), each with its own pair (ξ,Δ),(ξ′,Δ′) and weight νσa(Ea)νσb(Eb); a branch with |εσ|>W is dropped.

Step 2 — the product of the two gaps in closed form.

Multiplying the branch values of (12) for the two states (ℓa,ℓb their ℓ's):

ξaξb+ΔaΔb=Emin2+σaσbℓaℓb
(13)
ξaξb−ΔaΔb=−(1−b2)Δ*2+2bΔ*κ(σaℓa+σbℓb)+σaσb(1−b2)κ2ℓaℓb
(14)

Hence the coherence factors of (5) and (6) are explicit functions of Ea,Eb,σa,σb only:

Cσaσb(η=−1)=12[1+Emin2+σaσbℓaℓbEaEb],Cσaσb(η=+1)=12[1+ξaξb−ΔaΔbEaEb] with (14),Cp=12[1−ξaξb−ηΔaΔbEaEb]
(15)

BCS limit b→0: ξ*=0, Δ*=ΔF=Δ, κ=1, Emin=|Δ|, ξσ=σℓ, and (13), (14) reduce to Δ2+σaσbℓaℓb and −Δ2+σaσbℓaℓb: the standard case-II and case-I coherence factors. For b≠0 the extra terms are exactly the particle–hole asymmetry of the gap. Check: (12)–(14) hold to 1×10−14 over 20000 random (Ea,Eb,σa,σb).

4. Result

Collecting (5), (6), (15) and the normal-state absorption with its own chemical potential μN(T) at the same filling:

WWN=Wsc+WpairWN,WN=2πωB2Nk2∫dεN(ε)N(ε+ℏω)[fN(ε)−fN(ε+ℏω)]
(16)

Inputs: Δ0(T),Δs(T),μ(T) from the self-consistent solution of HHM Eqs. 32, 33, 38 (no Δeff); the lattice enters only through N(ε), the band half-width W and (via ε) the helicity bands. Assumptions: constant B and unrestricted 𝐤,𝐤′ sums (Sec. 2). Not covered: fixed q (clean limit), a direction-dependent B𝐪, and the q→0 electromagnetic interband vertex — these need the 𝐤-resolved form (8).

5. The sign of ξ in the coherence factor, and what to expect for W/WN

What the paper does. Its expansion of [uu′−ηvv′]2 (written with positive square roots, v≥0) gives 12(1+ξξ′/EE′−ΔΔ′/EE′) with the signed ξξ′ — the correct case-I scattering factor, and the paper remarks that this term cannot be dropped. Two later steps spoil it: (i) the final W carries +ηΔΔ′, whereas (uu′−ηvv′)2 gives −ηΔΔ′; (ii) “in terms of E” the paper replaces ε−μ by E2−Δ2. Squaring E2=ξ2+Δ2 erases the sign of ξ, and taking the positive root puts every state on the electron side: ξξ′→|ξ||ξ′|.

Why the sign of ξ cannot be dropped.

Write the Bogoliubov coefficients as u=cosφ, v=sinφ, with 2φ the polar angle of the vector (ξ,Δ¯) (both components signed):

cos2φ=ξE,sin2φ=Δ¯E,uu′−ηvv′=cos(φ+ηφ′),uv′+ηu′v=sin(φ′+ηφ)
(17)

Hence, for scattering and for pair creation,

C=cos2(φ+ηφ′)=12[1+ξξ′−ηΔ¯Δ¯′EE′],Cp=sin2(φ′+ηφ)=12[1−ξξ′−ηΔ¯Δ¯′EE′],C+Cp=1
(18)

For η=+1 (ultrasound) the combination is ξξ′−Δ¯Δ¯′ with ξ,Δ¯ signed. The sign of ξ is the electron/hole label: for Δ¯→0, φ=0 (ξ>0) or π/2 (ξ<0), so (18) gives C=1 for two states of the same type and C=0 for opposite types (a scalar vertex cannot turn an electron into a hole), and Cp is the complement — opposite-type pairs are created, not scattered. With |ξ||ξ′| one gets C=1 for every pair at Δ¯=0 and no pair creation. Sum rule at Δ¯→0 (scattering + pair creation must reproduce the normal-state absorption, T/Tc=0.7): signed ξξ′: 0.3507 + 0.6502 = 1.0008; with |ξ||ξ′|: 0.6436 + 0.0000 = 0.6436.

Does the choice change the behaviour of W/WN?

In the paper's (and the repo's) formula the bracket is 1+n·n′ with unit vectors n=(|ξ|,Δ)/E, n′=(|ξ′|,ηΔ′)/E′, hence lies in [0,2]; WN has bracket 2. Therefore W/WN≤1 identically, whatever the data: for the repo (bracket in [1.4966, 2.0000]) the ratio is the average of C=bracket/2∈[0.75,1] over the thermal window, 0.93–0.96. This bound is a property of the formula, not evidence for it. It disappears as soon as the sign of ξ is kept in the old integrand (band-energy conservation, normal-state f): normalised by its own Δ→0 limit, which vanishes for pairs straddling μ, the ratio becomes (T/Tc = 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.8): 16.01, 4.96, 2.80, 2.05, 1.74 with +ηΔ2, and 1.04, 0.54, 0.53, 0.64, 0.70 with −ηΔ2 (repo inputs, ℏω=0.7). That framework cannot be repaired by choosing signs: the pairs straddling μ need quasiparticle energy conservation and the pair-creation factor Cp.

For ℏω<2Δ only scattering contributes and Ws/Wn<1 (→2f(Δ) as ω→0, verified to 10−4). Above 2Δ the pair-creation factor Cp=sin2(φ+φ′) of case I jumps to ≈π/2 at the threshold (the two BCS edges, Cp→1) and decays to 1 from above; for η=−1 it rises continuously from 0. In our model (VSO=0.4082, correct formula, η=+1): ℏω=0.7: maximum 1.402 at T/Tc=0.9828, below the onset (T/Tc < 0.9137) maximum 0.339; ℏω=0.2: maximum 1.007 at T/Tc=0.9947, below the onset (T/Tc < 0.9939) maximum 0.767; ℏω=0.05: maximum 0.941 at T/Tc=0.9995, below the onset (none reached) maximum 0.941. At ℏω≳2Δ the density-type vertex is also where vertex corrections (gauge invariance) are expected to matter, which are not included.
