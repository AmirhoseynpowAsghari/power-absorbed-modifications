From Δ(ε) to Δ(E)

The gap function of the Rashba + correlated-hopping model as a function of the quasiparticle energy — step-by-step derivation, including the band test that discards unphysical roots
Paper: Joel Hutchinson, J. E. Hirsch, Frank Marsiglio, Enhancement of Superconducting Tc due to the Spin-orbit Interaction, Phys. Rev. B 97, 184513 (2018), arXiv:1801.08988. “Paper Eq. n” always refers to that article; the equations of this note are numbered (1), (2), …
Result. Let b≡Δs/4t, ΔF≡Δ0−μb, Emin≡|ΔF|/1+b2, ε*≡(μ+bΔ0)/(1+b2) and W≡4t1+VSO2/2t2 (half-width of the Rashba band). Solving E(ε)=E gives two mathematical branches, “+” with ε>ε* and “−” with ε<ε*, defined for E≥Emin:
Δ±(E)=ΔF1+b2∓b1+b2E2−Emin2
(R)
ε±(E)=ε*±E2−Emin21+b2
(R')
Only branches whose band energy lies inside the Rashba band are real states. The test, applied to each sign separately, is
−W≤ε±(E)≤+W
(A)
A branch with |ε±(E)|>W is discarded: no (𝐤,s) state has quasiparticle energy E on it, so Δ±(E) does not exist there. For ε* inside the band, Δ(E) is therefore two-valued only for Emin≤E≤min(E+W,E−W), single-valued up to max(E+W,E−W) and undefined above that, where E±W≡E(ε=±W) is the quasiparticle energy at the band edge (Sec. 3; Sec. 3.3 gives the case of ε* outside the band).

The paper's result is Δ(ε)=Δ0−Δsε/4t (paper Eq. 37). “Δ(E)” is taken to mean the gap of the state whose quasiparticle energy is E. If you instead meant E≡ε−μ, nothing needs inverting: (6) below is already Δ(E)=ΔF−bE (restricted, as always, to the band, −W≤E+μ≤W).

Notation clashes in the paper: g(E) is the density of states in paper Eq. 18 (there E is a band energy) and g(E)≡(1−2f(E))/2E after paper Eq. 34 (there E is the quasiparticle energy); Emin is the bottom of the free Rashba band in Sec. II and the minimum excitation energy in paper Eq. 40. Here N(ε) is the normal-state density of states (∫Ndε=2, both helicities), the band bottom is −W, and Emin is the minimum excitation energy of paper Eq. 40.

1. Starting point: why the gap depends on (k, s) only through ε

The paper's ansatz (paper Eq. 31), with s𝐤=12(coskx+cosky), is

Δ¯𝐤s=Δ0+Δss𝐤+Δx−ysR𝐤
(1)

Paper Eqs. 33 and 34 have the same 𝐤′-sum on the right-hand side, so their ratio gives (paper Eq. 35)

Δx−yΔs=4(Δt/t)VSO8Δt=VSO2t
(2)

Now use ε𝐤=−2t(coskx+cosky), i.e. s𝐤=−ε𝐤/4t, and paper Eq. 15, i.e. sVSOR𝐤=−(ε𝐤s−ε𝐤)/2:

s𝐤+sVSO2tR𝐤=−ε𝐤4t−ε𝐤s−ε𝐤4t=−ε𝐤s4t
(3)

Inserting (2) and (3) into (1) gives paper Eq. 37:

Δ¯𝐤s=Δ0−Δs4tε𝐤s
(4)

Since Δ𝐤s=se−iθ𝐤Δ¯𝐤s (paper, text after Eq. 22) differs from Δ¯𝐤s only by a phase, |Δ𝐤s|=|Δ¯𝐤s| and the quasiparticle energy depends on (𝐤,s) only through ε𝐤s:

E(ε)=(ε−μ)2+(Δ0−Δs4tε)2
(5)

All dependence on 𝐤, s and VSO therefore sits in ε𝐤s, in the density of states N(ε), in the band edges ±W (Sec. 3) and in the self-consistent numbers Δ0,Δs,μ. The inversion ε→E is a one-variable problem.

2. The inversion: two mathematical roots (Steps 1–5)

In Steps 1–5, ε is treated as an arbitrary real number. They give the two mathematical solutions of E(ε)=E; Step 6 (Sec. 3) then decides which of them are physical.

Step 1 — measure energies from the Fermi level.

Put ξ=ε−μ, b≡Δs/4t and ΔF≡Δ(ε=μ)=Δ0−bμ. Then

Δ(ξ)=Δ0−b(μ+ξ)=ΔF−bξ
(6)
Step 2 — write E2 as a polynomial in ξ.
E2=ξ2+(ΔF−bξ)2=(1+b2)ξ2−2bΔFξ+ΔF2
(7)

This is a convex parabola in ξ (leading coefficient 1+b2>0): E has exactly one minimum, and every larger E is reached at two values of ξ.

Step 3 — complete the square.
E2=Emin2+(1+b2)(ξ−ξ*)2,ξ*=bΔF1+b2,Emin=|ΔF|1+b2
(8)

Check: (1+b2)(ξ−ξ*)2=(1+b2)ξ2−2bΔFξ+b2ΔF2/(1+b2), and adding Emin2=ΔF2/(1+b2) gives the constant ΔF2, as in (7).

In the paper's variables, ε*=μ+ξ* and Emin read

ε*=μ+Δ0Δs/4t1+(Δs/4t)2,Emin=|Δ0−μΔs/4t|1+(Δs/4t)2
(9)

which are exactly paper Eqs. 39 and 40: a first consistency check. Because ξ*≠0 the minimum of E(ε) is not at the Fermi level.

Step 4 — solve for ξ.
ξ±(E)=ξ*±E2−Emin21+b2=bΔF±(1+b2)E2−ΔF21+b2,E≥Emin
(10)

There is no real root for E<Emin. The two roots, ε±=μ+ξ±, lie above and below ε*. They are only mathematical roots: whether a root corresponds to real states is decided in Step 6.

Step 5 — insert ξ±(E) into Δ(ξ)=ΔF−bξ.
Δ±(E)=ΔF−bξ±(E)=ΔF−b2ΔF1+b2∓b(1+b2)E2−ΔF21+b2
(11)

Using ΔF−b2ΔF/(1+b2)=ΔF/(1+b2) and (1+b2)E2−ΔF2=(1+b2)(E2−Emin2) this simplifies to

Δ±(E)=ΔF1+b2∓b1+b2E2−Emin2,E≥Emin
(R)
ε±(E)=μ+ξ±(E)=ε*±E2−Emin21+b2
(R')

Written out with the paper's Δ0,Δs,μ:

Δ±(E)=Δ0−μΔs4t∓Δs4t[1+(Δs4t)2]E2−(Δ0−μΔs4t)21+(Δs4t)2
(12)

Equivalent form with b=tanφ: Δ±(E)=ΔFcos2φ∓sinφE2−ΔF2cos2φ.

Geometric picture

In the (ξ,Δ) plane, (6) is a straight line and E=ξ2+Δ2 is the distance from the origin (Fig. 1b). Emin=|ΔF|/1+b2 is the perpendicular distance from the origin to the line, (ξ*,Δ*) is the foot of the perpendicular, and a circle of radius E>Emin cuts the line in two points — the two mathematical branches. Eq. (R) says: walk a distance E2−Emin2 along the line, in either direction, from the foot of the perpendicular. Only the part of the line that lies inside the band is physical (solid in Fig. 1b); intersections on the dotted part are the discarded roots.

3. Step 6 — keep only the roots that are real states (band test)

A root ε±(E) describes a physical quasiparticle only if some (𝐤,s) actually has that band energy, i.e. only if ε±(E) lies inside the Rashba band. This single question decides which branches survive.

3.1 The allowed band

−W≤ε𝐤s≤+W,W=4t1+VSO22t2
(13)

Proof. For s=−1, ε𝐤s=−2t(coskx+cosky)+2VSOR𝐤. With u=12(coskx+cosky)∈[−1,1] one has R𝐤2=2−cos2kx−cos2ky≤2(1−u2), hence, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

ε𝐤s≤−4tu+22VSO1−u2≤(4t)2+8VSO2=W

Equality holds on the diagonals |kx|=|ky|, so the maximum of ε𝐤s is exactly W; the s=+1 band is the mirror image, with minimum −W. Separately, the helicity bands are s=+1: [−W,4t] and s=−1: [−4t,W] (the points (π,π) and (0,0) give the ±4t ends). ε𝐤s is continuous on the Brillouin zone, so every energy in [−W,W] is realised and none outside. Because Δ¯ and E depend on (𝐤,s) only through ε𝐤s (Sec. 1), the helicity label plays no role in the test below.

3.2 The admissibility rule

−W≤εσ(E)≤+W,εσ(E)=ε*+σE2−Emin21+b2,σ=±1
(A)

Discard rule. If |εσ(E)|>W, no (𝐤,s) state has E𝐤s=E with ε𝐤s=εσ(E). That root is an artefact of continuing the parabola (7) to band energies that do not exist; it is discarded and Δσ(E) is undefined at that E (not zero, not small). It must not enter any plot, average, sum or integral. In Fig. 1 these are the open circles in (b) and the dashed parts of the curves in (a); in Figs. 2 and 3 the dashed lines.

3.3 The rule written as a condition on E

By (8), E grows monotonically with |ε−ε*|. A root therefore reaches a band edge exactly when E equals the quasiparticle energy at that edge,

E±W≡E(ε=±W)=Emin2+(1+b2)(±W−ε*)2=(±W−μ)2+(Δ0∓bW)2
(14)

For ε* inside the band (|ε*|≤W, the usual case) the test (A) is therefore equivalent to a simple energy window for each branch:

branchband energy of the rootreal (passes (A)) for
+ε+≥ε*Emin≤E≤E+W
−ε−≤ε*Emin≤E≤E−W

Consequently: (i) Δ(E) is two-valued only for Emin≤E≤min(E+W,E−W); (ii) for min(E+W,E−W)<E≤max(E+W,E−W) exactly one branch survives and Δ(E) is single-valued; (iii) above Emax≡max(E+W,E−W) there are no states and Δ(E) does not exist (Emax is the largest quasiparticle energy in the whole zone).

If ε* lies outside the band (e.g. μ near a band edge together with large b), the Emin of paper Eq. 40 is not reached by any state and the physical threshold moves to a band-edge energy. The general windows are

branchreal fornever real if
+E(max(ε*,−W))≤E≤E+Wε*>W
−E(min(ε*,W))≤E≤E−Wε*<−W

with E(ε) from (5); for |ε*|≤W they reduce to the previous table.

4. Checks and properties

  • BCS limit b→0 (energy-independent gap): Δ±(E)→ΔF, Emin→|ΔF|, ε±→μ±E2−ΔF2.
  • Threshold E=Emin (for |ε*|≤W): the root vanishes, both branches meet at Δ*=ΔF/(1+b2)=Δ(ε*).
  • High energy: Δ±(E)→Δ*∓b1+b2E (there E≈|ξ|1+b2): each formula branch grows linearly with E — within the band, up to E±W.
  • Branch splitting (particle–hole asymmetry), where both branches are real: Δ−(E)−Δ+(E)=2b1+b2E2−Emin2, zero for b=0.
  • Back-substitution: ξ±2+Δ±2=E2 identically (checked symbolically with sympy, and numerically for every (𝐤,s) state, Fig. 3).
  • Emin=0 only if ΔF=0, i.e. if the node ε0=4tΔ0/Δs of the gap function lies exactly at μ.
  • Band test, numerically. At VSO=0.5t the helicity bands are s=+1: [−4.2426, 4.0000]t and s=−1: [−4.0000, 4.2426]t, so W=4.2426t. Over all 28,800 states, branch + occupies exactly E∈ [0.8991, 1.2327]t and branch − exactly [0.8991, 8.1494]t: the windows of the table above (Emin=0.8991t, E+W=1.233t, E−W=8.149t); no state sits on a discarded root. Stress test: 300 random sets of (Δ0,Δs,μ,VSO), 35 of them with ε* outside the band, brute force on a 300×300×2 grid — no state lies outside the predicted window of its branch, and the upper end of every window (branch reaching a band edge) is filled to within 2×10−4 t (grid resolution).

5. Recipe for computing Δ(E)

  1. Get Δ0,Δs,μ for your T,U,Δt,n,VSO from the self-consistent solution of paper Eqs. 32, 33, 38 (Δx−y=VSOΔs/2t then follows). T, U, Δt and n enter only through these three numbers; VSO also enters W.
  2. Compute b=Δs/4t, ΔF=Δ0−μb, Emin=|ΔF|/1+b2, ε*=(μ+bΔ0)/(1+b2), Δ*=ΔF/(1+b2) and W=4t1+VSO2/2t2.
  3. For a given E: if E<Emin there is no state (and none if E>Emax).
  4. Otherwise put ℓ=E2−Emin2, ε±=ε*±ℓ/1+b2 and Δ±=Δ*∓bℓ/1+b2 — the two mathematical branches.
  5. Band test: keep a branch only if |ε±|≤W; discard every branch with |ε±|>W.
  6. What remains (zero, one or two values) is Δ(E). It keeps the sign of Δ¯: it vanishes at ε0=4tΔ0/Δs, i.e. at E0=|ε0−μ| on the branch that contains ε0.

6. By-products you will probably need next

Differentiating (10) gives the Jacobian, which turns a 𝐤-sum into an energy integral. With S(E)={σ:|εσ(E)|≤W}, the set of branches that pass the band test (A):

|dε±dE|=E(1+b2)E2−ΔF2=E1+b2E2−Emin2
(15)

so the quasiparticle density of states has a BCS-like inverse-square-root edge at Emin, and the sum runs only over admissible branches:

Ns(E)=∑σ∈S(E)N(εσ(E))|dεσdE|
(16)

With the BCS coherence factors v2=12(1−ξ/E) (paper Eq. 24) and u2=1−v2, the T=0 tunnelling density of states (momentum-independent matrix element, spin summed; η=+1 adds an electron, weight u2, η=−1 removes one, weight v2) is

Nt(ηE)=∑σ∈S(E)N(εσ(E))E+ηξσ(E)2(1+b2)E2−ΔF2
(17)

N(ε) vanishes outside the band, but the closed forms above are finite on a discarded root, so in code the test (A) must be applied explicitly. At the gap edge (for |ε*|≤W, so that both branches are present) ξ±→ξ* and the two coherence-peak weights are in the ratio

Nt(+Emin)Nt(−Emin)=1+β1−β,β=ξ*Emin=sgn(ΔF)b1+b2
(18)

This is, in closed form, the density-of-states-independent asymmetry between bias polarities that the paper points out after Eq. 37. For the example below β=−0.316, the ratio is 0.520, i.e. the η=−1 edge is 1.92 times stronger than the η=+1 edge.

8. Minimal code and files

import numpy as np

def delta_of_E(E, D0, Ds, mu, VSO, t=1.0):
    """Physical branches of Delta(E): (Delta_plus, Delta_minus).
    NaN for E < Emin and where the root eps_pm lies outside the band (|eps| > W)."""
    E = np.asarray(E, float)
    b     = Ds / (4*t)
    DF    = D0 - b*mu                               # Delta at the Fermi level
    s     = np.sqrt(1 + b*b)
    W     = 4*t*np.sqrt(1 + VSO**2/(2*t**2))        # Rashba band:  -W <= eps_ks <= W
    Emin  = abs(DF)/s                               # paper Eq. 40
    estar = (mu + b*D0)/(1 + b*b)                   # paper Eq. 39
    Dstar = DF/(1 + b*b)                            # Delta(eps*)
    with np.errstate(invalid="ignore"):
        ell   = np.sqrt(E**2 - Emin**2)             # NaN below the threshold
        eps_p = estar + ell/s                       # band energy of the "+" root
        eps_m = estar - ell/s                       # band energy of the "-" root
        Dp = np.where(np.abs(eps_p) <= W, Dstar - b*ell/s, np.nan)   # band test (A)
        Dm = np.where(np.abs(eps_m) <= W, Dstar + b*ell/s, np.nan)   # discarded -> NaN
    return Dp, Dm
